Preparing a phone for repair has two separate goals. You want a recoverable copy of important data in case the phone is erased, and you want to limit access to your everyday personal information while someone else handles the device. A repair mode can help with privacy, but it does not replace a backup.

Read the instructions for the repair you booked before changing the phone. Preparation depends on the model, the work being done and the provider receiving it.

If the battery is swelling, leaking, producing an unusual odor or getting excessively hot, stop using and charging the phone rather than trying to finish a backup. The [U.S. Fire Administration identifies these as stop-use signs](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prevent-fires/batteries/). Ask the manufacturer or repair provider for safe handling instructions.

## Make sure the important data exists somewhere else

Start with the information that would be difficult or impossible to recreate: recent photos, documents, recordings, messages and data stored inside important apps. Check the backup destination itself, not only a status icon on the phone. You also need to know that you can sign in to the account holding the backup.

For Google's device backup, open the backup settings and review **Backup details**. The [Android backup guide](https://support.google.com/android/answer/2819582?hl=en) explains how to inspect included data and run a manual backup through Settings > Google > All services > Backup and restore > Backup > Back up now. Labels may differ on your phone.

Google also warns that not every app can restore all of its data, and photo and video backup through Google Photos is handled separately. For an app whose contents matter to you, check that app's own backup or export instructions rather than treating a general device-backup status as proof that everything is covered.

When you copy files manually, open a few of the copies on the destination device before relying on them. If a computer only charges the phone, [check the USB file-transfer steps](/guides/android-usb-charges-but-no-file-transfer/) before assuming the copies were made. Keep that destination under your control. A repair provider needs the phone and the information relevant to the fault, not a folder containing your entire private backup.

If the display or touch input is already unusable, check existing backups from another device and tell the repair provider what you could not verify. Do not approve a reset on the assumption that a backup must exist somewhere.

## Plan how you will sign in without the phone

A password may not be enough if an account normally asks this phone to approve a sign-in or receive a code. Before handing it over, check how you would complete that second step from another device.

For Google accounts that offer them, [backup codes](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/1187538?hl=en) can provide an alternative second step when the normal method is unavailable. Each code works once. Store the codes securely somewhere other than only on the phone being repaired, and do not give them to the technician. Google's Advanced Protection Program does not offer downloadable backup codes, so use the recovery options available for your own account.

Other services have their own recovery methods. Check the ones you depend on while you still have access, without weakening account security just to simplify the repair appointment.

## Use a repair mode when your phone provides one

Repair and maintenance modes create a restricted environment for the technician instead of opening your normal user profile. They still do not guarantee that data will survive the repair. A reset or storage replacement can erase it, so manufacturers continue to recommend a backup.

### Pixel Repair Mode

Google documents [Repair Mode](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/14266732?hl=en) for eligible Pixel phones running the Android 14 December 2023 update or later, with at least 2 GB of free space. Open Settings > System > Repair mode, follow the prompts and let the phone restart.

Use your existing screen-lock credential to enter the mode. Google specifically says not to share that credential with the technician. It also says information generated inside Repair Mode is removed when you exit, so do not create your only copy of a repair note or test photo there.

### Galaxy Maintenance mode

On supported Galaxy devices, [Samsung's Maintenance mode](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10003675/) uses a separate user account to keep the normal personal profile away from the repair session. Samsung lists One UI 5 or later as a requirement, with availability varying by device configuration. In Settings, open Device care > Maintenance mode, then Turn on and follow the restart prompts. A screen lock is required.

Samsung offers backup options alongside Maintenance mode, including temporary cloud backup and external storage where supported. Check what the selected option covers before relying on it. Samsung's temporary cloud backup is kept for 30 days, so it is not a long-term archive.

Confirm that the phone has entered the intended repair or maintenance mode before handing it over.

## Agree on resets, SIMs and accessories before handover

If your phone has no suitable repair mode, or the provider says the job requires a reset, ask for the preparation steps for that exact device. A [factory reset erases the phone's data](https://support.google.com/android/answer/6088915). Verify your copies and account access before agreeing to one.

Tell the provider when preserving data matters and which preparation steps you could not complete. This is especially important when a broken display prevents you from enabling a mode or checking a fresh backup yourself.

Follow the booking instructions for physical SIM cards, memory cards and accessories. Google's Repair Mode guide tells Pixel owners to keep their physical SIM rather than send it with the phone. An eSIM needs different handling, so ask about service continuity instead of deleting its profile as a precaution.

Keep the repair reference, fault description and any example the technician genuinely needs somewhere you can reach without the phone. For a fault that comes and goes, [prepare a short incident record](/guides/document-an-intermittent-android-problem/) rather than trying to force it to happen again.

Before the handover, check three things:

1. You know where the important copies are and have opened enough of them to trust the backup.
2. You know how to sign in to essential accounts without relying only on the phone being repaired.
3. You know what the repair provider expects you to send, remove, reset or leave in place.
