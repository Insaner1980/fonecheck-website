Open a battery screen and you may find percentages, temperatures, voltage and a status such as “Good.” They look precise, but they do not all answer the same question.

fonecheck brings together the battery information Android makes available on your device. Understanding the meaning of each reading is more useful than treating the screen as a single verdict.

## Charge is a current reading

The charge percentage describes the reported charge level now. It is not a measurement of how much original capacity remains.

For example, the supplied fonecheck preview shows 88% charge. That number does not mean the battery has 88% of its original capacity, and it is not a prediction of the hours left before charging.

## Charging state adds context

Charging state tells you whether the phone reports that it is charging or discharging. Other available fields can include the power source, voltage and current.

An instantaneous current reading is a momentary observation. In the fonecheck Pixel preview, it is explicitly labelled with low confidence. Keep that qualification attached to the number when you interpret or share it.

Information can differ between manufacturers and Android versions. A missing field should remain missing rather than being treated as a zero or a fault.

## Temperature needs a moment in time

The battery temperature field is the temperature Android reports for the battery. It does not describe every component inside the phone.

Keep the circumstances in mind when reviewing it: was the phone charging, running a check or sitting idle? A saved observation records a particular moment. It does not prove how the phone behaves throughout the day.

fonecheck also has a separate Thermal area for available platform thermal status. Battery temperature and the platform’s thermal status are related pieces of context, but they are not interchangeable measurements.

## “Good” is not a capacity certificate

The Android-reported battery status can say “Good.” In fonecheck, the accompanying explanation makes the limit explicit: this is Android’s current status value, not a battery capacity or wear diagnosis.

Likewise, a cycle count, when available, should be read as one piece of reported information. fonecheck does not turn it into a universal battery-life guarantee.
