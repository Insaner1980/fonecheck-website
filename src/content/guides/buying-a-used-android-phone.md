A phone can look great in a listing and still leave you with questions. Does touch respond near the edges? Can the other person hear you clearly? Does the camera focus when you need it to?

Give yourself time to look, listen and try the things you use every day. This checklist is a starting point for that conversation, rather than a guarantee about a device’s condition.

## Start with the phone in front of you

Compare the model and storage information in the phone’s settings with the listing. Look at the display, frame, cameras, buttons and charging port. Ask the seller about repairs and any behaviour they have noticed.

A diagnostic app can help organise observations, but it cannot establish ownership or reveal every repair. Keep questions about the phone’s history separate from the results of a hardware check.

## Give the screen your attention

Open a few different screens and look across the entire display. Try a light background and a dark one. Look for areas that seem uneven, lines that remain in place or pixels that look different from those around them.

Test touch near the edges as well as the centre. Type a short message and try gestures you regularly use. A guided touch grid can help you work across the display instead of repeatedly tapping the same area.

## Listen, speak and take a photo

With the seller’s agreement, play a sound and listen for anything unexpected. Record a short microphone sample and play it back. Open the front and rear cameras and take a test photo.

Check these functions in a setting where you can judge them. A noisy shop can make an audio problem harder to hear. A camera specification says what is reported as supported; using the camera shows you how it behaves during your check.

## Read battery information in context

Charge percentage tells you how much charge the phone reports right now. It does not tell you how long the battery will last in your daily use or how much its original capacity has changed.

Ask about everyday battery life and charging behaviour. Treat the battery information available through Android as one part of the picture. Our [battery guide](/guides/understanding-android-battery-information/) explains the distinction between a reading and a capacity assessment.

## Keep unanswered questions visible

If you cannot test something, write that down. A skipped test, a missing permission and a confirmed problem are different outcomes.

A fonecheck report can help keep these observations together. When looking at an example or a shared report, check its date, its scope and its coverage as well as its score. A high score can coexist with untested features.

Before deciding, return to the functions that matter to you. A useful check gives you better questions to ask and a clearer record of what you actually tried.
