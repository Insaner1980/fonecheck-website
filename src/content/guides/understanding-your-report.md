A report is most useful when you can see both what it says and what it leaves open. fonecheck keeps the overall score, test coverage and individual observations separate so you can read them together.

Full Check and saved reports are planned features of the paid version. The app is coming to Google Play in fall 2026. The example values below come from a pre-release app screenshot.

## Start with scope and date

First, check whether the report describes a Full Check or a retest of one diagnostic area. A category retest creates a new report without changing the older one.

Next, check when it was made. A report describes the observations saved during that check. It is not a live status page for everything that has happened since.

## Read score and coverage together

The score summarises passed, warning and failed observations. Coverage describes the completion of available, applicable observations, including information-only results. Unavailable or inapplicable observations are excluded from that coverage calculation.

In the example report, the score is **96 out of 100** and coverage is **85%**, labelled **Partial coverage**. Those numbers answer different questions. The score is not “96% of the phone has been tested,” and neither number is a guarantee of the phone’s condition.

Even full coverage does not mean every possible hardware issue has been ruled out. Read the findings and their limits alongside the summary.

## Open the findings that need attention

A summary points you towards a question; the details help you understand it. Check the observation’s description, source and any confidence or availability information before drawing a conclusion.

For example, a connectivity observation may say that a function is disabled. That context matters. A function being disabled during the check is different from evidence that its hardware is broken.

Some checks use Android-provided information. Others depend on you confirming something you saw, heard or felt. That distinction should stay visible when you interpret the result.

## Keep untested features separate from faults

An unavailable test may reflect hardware support, Android restrictions or missing permissions. A test that was not performed remains untested. Neither outcome should silently become a pass or a confirmed fault.

If a particular feature matters to you, look for an observation that actually covers it. A strong overall result cannot replace an unanswered question about the camera, touchscreen or another specific function.

## Save, compare and share deliberately

Reports are saved locally on the phone. You can reopen them, delete them, and compare compatible pairs: two Full Check reports, or two retests of the same category.

The planned full version also includes PDF and JSON export. You choose whether to share the exported file. Review its contents before sending it, since a diagnostic report contains information about your device.

A shared report is a record of findings, not a verified condition certificate. Keeping its date, scope, coverage and limitations together makes it much more useful to the person reading it.
